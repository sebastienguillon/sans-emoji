# Sans Emoji 2026.10.15

Prepared on October 15, 2026. Changes since `v2026.10.10`, including the abandoned [2026.10.14 preparation](2026.10.14.md). Version 2026.10.14 will not be tagged or published. Store submission, approval, and signing are separate steps.

## Fixes

- Prevent hidden emoji suggestions from briefly expanding the page or a scroll container and causing a scrollbar or layout jump while they are created and removed.
- Keep modern emoji popups suppressed when they appear after their inline query disappears or remain mounted during cancellation. The typed colon stays visible, and legacy popup cancellation remains supported.
- Cancel a query field that becomes editable after it mounts.
- In Firefox, update an already-rendered suggestion panel when a colon query's decoration mark is inserted or removed after another element in its paragraph, including after bold text or before the paragraph's trailing break. Previously the panel could stay visible when the query mounted, or stay hidden after it was removed.

## Performance

- Track emoji query fields and popups from mutation records instead of searching the document on every keystroke, focus change, and composition end, or searching an entire subtree whenever an element's class or style changes.
- Narrow the stylesheet's document-wide fallback to `data-query-prefix`, avoiding Firefox's repeated whole-document checks when unrelated editable node views change. Keep declarative suppression active from the first frame, including delayed cancellation and IME composition.
- Earlier synthetic measurements on a 29,164-element page found typing overhead fell from about 1 ms to 0.002 ms or less per keystroke in Chromium, and from about 0.5 ms to under 0.04 ms in Firefox. Firefox's style overhead during subtree churn after a menu had been used fell from 28–30 ms to about 0.2 ms per frame. These are headless fixture measurements, not live-tenant benchmarks; Chromium's corresponding style cost is unchanged. See the [measurement context](../README.md#performance-considerations).

## Known limitations and submission checks

- Live signed-in Jira, Confluence, and Bitbucket editors have not been checked with the final build. Before store submission, complete punctuation spacing variants, Enter/Tab, undo/redo, mentions, slash commands, and the toolbar picker in a signed-in editor. The earlier Jira layout check does not cover that matrix.
- Chromium fixtures load the unpacked extension; Firefox fixtures inject its compiled script and stylesheet. They do not verify final Firefox add-on installation or signing. The Chrome 120 and Firefox 140 minimum versions have not been tested; desktop Firefox 140+ is required and Android distribution is not enabled.
- An editor that ignores synthetic Escape may retain an invisible autocomplete state that consumes Enter or Tab. Legacy editors that handle Escape above the focused field may need an adapter.
- The modern hiding rule applies throughout the document while any `[data-query-prefix=":"]` element exists. Atlaskit sets that attribute on its contenteditable query field; simultaneous menus are not independently scoped.
- A suggestion panel without emoji items, such as Atlaskit's error fallback, can paint after its query disappears and until Atlaskit unmounts it. Internal markup changes can affect support; custom domains and unrelated frame URLs remain outside scope.
- Reviewers need working dedicated test credentials and an editable sample page, supplied privately through the store dashboard. The archive is unsigned; preparing and pushing a Git tag does not submit a store update or request signing.
